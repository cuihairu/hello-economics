# The historical progression of public finance

This is not a table of contents but the chain of advance in public finance theory. The core is not memorising concepts but watching public finance answer, step by step: why does the state exist, why does the state spend more and more, and why did taxation, government debt and the budget become the core instruments of modern state governance.

## The problem tree and the progress of finding answers

> How to read: record each step as "advance the question -> short answer -> corresponding chapter"; where one step touches several places in the textbooks, list them all.

### Big question 1: Why does the state exist, and where does the legitimacy of fiscal activity come from

- Sub-question 1.1: Why the market cannot solve all problems.
  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Functions of public finance](/finance/财政职能)
- Sub-question 1.2: What basic functions public finance actually bears.
  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Functions of public finance](/finance/财政职能)
- The progress of finding the answer:
  1. The classical fiscal view first confined the answer to: the state manages only the most basic order.
     Short answer: early fiscal views stressed a small state, finance serving mainly national defence, justice and basic order.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Functions of public finance](/finance/财政职能)
  2. Market failure and the problem of public goods forced the theory to be corrected: the state cannot withdraw completely.
     Short answer: when the market cannot provide public goods efficiently and handle externalities, the state must intervene.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Functions of public finance](/finance/财政职能)
  3. The theory of fiscal functions advanced further to: finance bears the three functions of allocating resources, distributing income and stabilising the economy.
     Short answer: finance does not merely raise and spend money; it also bears the three functions of allocation, distribution and stabilisation.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Functions of public finance](/finance/财政职能)
  4. The conclusion of this round: finance is not an ancillary revenue-and-expenditure affair but a basic instrument of state governance.
     Short answer: finance has risen from the state's revenue and expenditure activity into an instrument of state governance and public policy.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Functions of public finance](/finance/财政职能)

### Big question 2: Why the state spends more and more, and why fiscal expenditure turned from sustaining the state to intervening in development

- Sub-question 2.1: Why fiscal expenditure expands continuously with modernisation.
  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 2 · The scale and structure of fiscal expenditure](/finance/财政支出规模与结构)
- Sub-question 2.2: Where the state's money mainly goes.
  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 2 · The scale and structure of fiscal expenditure](/finance/财政支出规模与结构)
- Sub-question 2.3: Why the state should invest directly and take on responsibility for social security.
  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 3 · Fiscal investment spending and social security spending](/finance/财政投资支出和社会保障支出)
- The progress of finding the answer:
  1. Wagner explained first: modern state fiscal expenditure expands continuously.
     Short answer: as modernisation advances, the scale of state expenditure tends to expand over the long run.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 2 · The scale and structure of fiscal expenditure](/finance/财政支出规模与结构)
  2. This still explained only the trend, not the use.
     Short answer: knowing that expenditure will expand is not enough to say where that money should actually go.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 2 · The scale and structure of fiscal expenditure](/finance/财政支出规模与结构)
  3. Keynesianism advanced further: fiscal expenditure can be used actively to stabilise the economy and promote development.
     Short answer: fiscal expenditure is not only the cost of keeping the state running; it can also be an instrument of demand management and development.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 2 · The scale and structure of fiscal expenditure](/finance/财政支出规模与结构) · [Chapter 3 · Fiscal investment spending and social security spending](/finance/财政投资支出和社会保障支出)
  4. Social security theory pushed expenditure further to: security, fairness and the sharing of risk.
     Short answer: state expenditure also bears the functions of social security, redistribution and risk sharing.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 3 · Fiscal investment spending and social security spending](/finance/财政投资支出和社会保障支出)
  5. The conclusion of this round: fiscal expenditure has moved from sustaining the state's running to intervening in development and safeguarding livelihoods.
     Short answer: modern fiscal expenditure already covers several goals — development, stabilisation and the safeguarding of livelihoods.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 2 · The scale and structure of fiscal expenditure](/finance/财政支出规模与结构) · [Chapter 3 · Fiscal investment spending and social security spending](/finance/财政投资支出和社会保障支出)

### Big question 3: How the state raises funds steadily and continuously

- Sub-question 3.1: Why the state may levy tax, and what principles taxation should follow.
  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 4 · Principles of taxation](/finance/税收原理)
- Sub-question 3.2: How the principles of taxation settle into a concrete tax system.
  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 5 · Tax systems](/finance/税收制度)
- Sub-question 3.3: Why the state cannot rely on tax alone but must also run on borrowing.
  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 6 · Government debt theory and management](/finance/国债理论与管理)
- The progress of finding the answer:
  1. Taxation theory answered first: where fiscal revenue comes from, and how to levy it reasonably.
     Short answer: taxation is the state's most basic source of revenue, and the key lies in fairness, efficiency and collectability.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 4 · Principles of taxation](/finance/税收原理) · [Chapter 5 · Tax systems](/finance/税收制度)
  2. But the modern state soon found: tax alone is not enough to meet war, crisis and large-scale investment.
     Short answer: facing intertemporal expenditure pressure, current revenue alone is often insufficient.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 5 · Tax systems](/finance/税收制度) · [Chapter 6 · Government debt theory and management](/finance/国债理论与管理)
  3. Government debt theory then advanced to: finance is not only a question of current revenue but also of intertemporal fundraising.
     Short answer: the state can allocate fiscal resources across time by borrowing.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 6 · Government debt theory and management](/finance/国债理论与管理)
  4. The conclusion of this round: taxation and government debt together form the modern system of fiscal revenue.
     Short answer: the modern system of fiscal revenue rests on both taxation and debt financing.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 4 · Principles of taxation](/finance/税收原理) · [Chapter 5 · Tax systems](/finance/税收制度) · [Chapter 6 · Government debt theory and management](/finance/国债理论与管理)

  4. The conclusion of this round: the core of modern finance is not only raising and spending money but institutional constraint and sustainability.

## 1. Question 1: Why the state exists, and where the legitimacy of fiscal activity comes from

### Late 18th to 19th century: the classical liberal fiscal view

- Representative works:
  traceable to Smith's fiscal thought and to classical liberal discussion of the state's boundary.
- Influenced by whom:
  it inherited classical economics and laissez-faire thought.
- What it proposed:
  a fiscal view of the small state, of the night-watchman state.
- What it was explaining:
  how far state finance ought to reach.
- Core view:
  the state is mainly responsible for national defence, justice and basic public order, and finance should be as restrained as possible.
- Progress relative to what came before:
  it raised finance from the crown's revenue and expenditure into a theoretical question of "the boundary of state function".
- What it had not solved:
  it could not explain the more complex realities of public goods, externalities and income distribution.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Functions of public finance](/finance/财政职能)

### Late 19th to early 20th century: the development of public finance theory

- Transitional figures:
  Pigou, Wicksell, Lindahl, Musgrave and others progressively systematised the analysis of public finance.
- Influenced by whom:
  built on the classical fiscal view, but gradually facing the reality of market failure and of expanding public needs.
- What it proposed:
  a systematic discussion of public goods, public choice and the functions of finance.
- What it was explaining:
  why the market cannot cover all problems of resource allocation, and why the state must intervene.
- Core view:
  finance is not only an instrument for raising money but also bears the three functions of allocating resources, distributing income and stabilising the economy.
- Progress relative to the classical fiscal view:
  it pushed "whether the state should exist" forward to "what the state should specifically do".
- What it had not solved:
  state intervention itself brings rent-seeking, inefficiency and government failure.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Functions of public finance](/finance/财政职能)

## 2. Question 2: Why the state spends more and more, and why fiscal expenditure turned from sustaining the state to intervening in development

### Late 19th century: Wagner, the thought of expanding fiscal expenditure

- Representative works:
  Wagner's related discussions of the growth trend in public expenditure.
- Influenced by whom:
  by observation of industrialisation and of the expansion of the state's functions in reality.
- What it proposed:
  a general judgement on the trend of expanding fiscal expenditure, often called Wagner's law.
- What it was explaining:
  why the fiscal scale of the modern state keeps expanding.
- Core view:
  as the economy develops and society becomes more complex, the state's share of expenditure rises.
- Progress relative to the small-state fiscal view:
  fiscal expansion is no longer treated as abnormal but seen as a structural result of the modernisation process.
- What it had not solved:
  it explained "why it expands", but had not yet made clear "how the expanding money should be spent efficiently".
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [The scale and structure of fiscal expenditure](/finance/财政支出规模与结构)

### 20th century: expenditure finance under the influence of Keynesianism

- Whom it directly corrected:
  it directly corrected the classical balanced budget and the traditional beliefs in a "small state, little expenditure".
- Influenced by whom:
  directly by Keynes's thought of macro demand management.
- What it proposed:
  fiscal expenditure as an instrument of stabilising the economy and promoting growth.
- What it was explaining:
  why the state should regulate the economy through investment, social security and transfers.
- Core view:
  fiscal expenditure is not only the cost of keeping the state running but also an instrument of macro control and of development strategy.
- Progress relative to Wagner:
  Wagner explained "expenditure will expand"; Keynesianism went further and explained "expenditure can be used actively to stabilise and develop the economy".
- What it had not solved:
  how to ensure the efficiency of fiscal investment and social security spending, and whether the expansion of expenditure brings a long-run burden and crowding-out problems.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [The scale and structure of fiscal expenditure](/finance/财政支出规模与结构) · [Fiscal investment spending and social security spending](/finance/财政投资支出和社会保障支出)

## 3. Question 3: How the state raises funds steadily and continuously

### From the classical principles of taxation to modern tax theory

- Representative works:
  Smith's four principles of taxation and the subsequent modern public finance literature.
- Influenced by whom:
  starting from Adam Smith's four principles of taxation and gradually moving into modern tax system design.
- What it proposed:
  analyses of tax fairness, efficiency, neutrality, shifting and the structure of tax rates.
- What it was explaining:
  why the state may levy tax, and how to levy it more reasonably.
- Core view:
  taxation is not only a source of fiscal revenue but also an institutional arrangement that affects incentives, distribution and the allocation of resources.
- Progress relative to the early fiscal view:
  it moved from "should the state levy tax at all" to "how to design the tax system more reasonably".
- What it had not solved:
  the tension between fairness and efficiency always exists and cannot be settled once and for all.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Principles of taxation](/finance/税收原理) · [Tax systems](/finance/税收制度)

### 20th century: the structuring of the modern tax system

- Influenced by whom:
  built on the principles of taxation, and also driven by the practice of modern state governance and of the welfare state.
- What it proposed:
  a composite tax system in which classified income tax, comprehensive income tax, turnover tax and income tax coexist.
- What it was explaining:
  why the modern state's tax system is multi-tiered and divided by function.
- Core view:
  different tax types carry different goals, and the tax system is a whole structure rather than a single tax type.
- Progress relative to the stage of taxation principles:
  it moved from the analysis of principles to institutional design.
- What it had not solved:
  tax complexity brings collection costs, tax avoidance and the difficulty of reform.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Tax systems](/finance/税收制度)

### Modern theory of debt finance

- Transitional figures:
  Buchanan and others, in discussing public debt and fiscal constraint, made "whether borrowing is reasonable" an independent question.
- Influenced by whom:
  built on war finance, Keynesianism and the development of modern financial markets.
- What it proposed:
  government debt financing, debt regulation and analyses of debt limits.
- What it was explaining:
  why the state cannot rely on current revenue alone but also runs on borrowing.
- Core view:
  government debt is both a financing instrument and a macro-control instrument, but the scale of debt must be constrained.
- Progress relative to purely tax-based finance:
  it moved finance from "balancing current revenue and expenditure" to "intertemporal allocation of resources".
- What it had not solved:
  when debt is a reasonable regulation and when it becomes an accumulation of risk remains in dispute.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Government debt theory and management](/finance/国债理论与管理)

## 4. Question 4: Why modern finance must operate institutionally

### Since the 20th century: the formation of the budget state

- Influenced by whom:
  by the development of modern democratic politics, of the rule-of-law state and of the institutionalisation of public governance.
- What it proposed:
  the institutional frames of the government budget, the budget management system and the budget law.
- What it was explaining:
  why finance cannot rely on ad hoc revenue and expenditure but must enter an institutionalised procedure.
- Core view:
  the budget is the hub of fiscal operation and also an instrument of state governance.
- Progress relative to traditional finance:
  it moved finance from "the revenue and expenditure of funds" to "institutional governance".
- What it had not solved:
  the budget system cannot automatically eliminate information asymmetry, local incentives and the soft budget constraint.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Government budget and the budget management system](/finance/国家预算与预算管理体制)

### Deficit finance and the debate over fiscal balance

- Influenced by whom:
  directly by the policy practice of Keynesianism and post-Keynesianism, and also constrained by debt sustainability.
- What it proposed:
  the analysis of budget deficits, structural deficits, cyclical deficits and fiscal balance.
- What it was explaining:
  why a deficit is sometimes a policy instrument and sometimes a signal of risk.
- Core view:
  fiscal balance cannot be mechanically understood as revenue equal to expenditure every year; the key is to look at the nature of the deficit, the economic cycle and the debt-bearing capacity.
- Progress relative to the traditional balanced fiscal view:
  it moved from static annual balance to dynamic, cyclical and intertemporal balance.
- What it had not solved:
  how to hold the balance between short-run stabilisation and long-run sustainability remains the core difficulty of public finance.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Fiscal balance and fiscal deficit](/finance/财政平衡与财政赤字)

## 5. Finally, compressing the whole book into one sentence

- The order of theoretical advance in public finance is:
  first explain why the state exists, then why the modern state has to spend more and more, then how the money is raised and the debt borrowed, and lastly why that money must operate through the budget system.
