# Bibliography

> When I put the main text together I worked from open textbooks and lecture notes; the chapter structure and the statements of the theories are checked mainly against the standard textbooks below.
> For the lineage of the theories, see also the [theory timeline](/timeline) and the "how the theory was born and how it was tested" sections of each subject Readme.

## Western economics

- Gao Hongye, *Western Economics* (8th edition, separate micro and macro volumes, China Renmin University Press) — the main source of the chapter framework: fifteen chapters from supply and demand to the disagreements between macro schools, which is exactly this system.
- Liang Xiaomin, *Introduction to Western Economics* (4th edition) — the entry-level supplement.
- Mankiw, *Principles of Economics* — another telling of the same system, with richer cases.

## Money and banking

- Cao Longqi, *Finance* (6th edition) — the standard framework for the chapters on money supply and demand, interest rates, the financial system and policy.
- Mishkin, *The Economics of Money, Banking, and Financial Markets* — the empirical supplement on money demand and monetary transmission.

## Public finance

- Chen Gong, *Public Finance* (10th edition) — the source of the structure across fiscal functions, expenditure, taxation, government debt, budget and deficit.

## International economics

- Krugman and Obstfeld, *International Economics* — the mainstream framework for trade theory and the open macroeconomy.
- Gao Hongye, *Western Economics* (macro part) — the domestic-textbook wording for the balance of payments and exchange rate chapters.

## Socialist economic theory

- Wei Xinghua and Zhang Yu, *Socialist Economic Theory* (Higher Education Press) — the standard framework from the introduction through government regulation.
- Wu Jinglian, *China's Economic Reform in the Contemporary Era* — the empirical narrative of institutional change and reform history.

## Mathematical foundations

- Tongji University, *Advanced Mathematics* — the standard treatment of limits, differentiation and integration.
- The linear algebra and probability-statistics parts follow the chapter split of the standard domestic textbooks (Tongji's *Linear Algebra*, Sheng Zhou's *Probability Theory and Mathematical Statistics*).

## History of economic thought and people

- Henry William Spiegel, *The Growth of Economic Thought* — the historical reference for the timeline and the economist entries.
- Mark Blaug, *Economic Theory in Retrospect* — theoretical lineage and critical commentary.

## Primary sources (cited where the chapters touch them)

- Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations* (1776)
- Marshall, *Principles of Economics* (1890)
- Keynes, *The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money* (1936)
- Friedman and Schwartz, *A Monetary History of the United States* (1963)
- Hayek, "The Use of Knowledge in Society" (1945)
- Coase, "The Problem of Social Cost" (1960)

- The old reading notes that go with this list are kept separately: [introduction to western economics notes](/reference/notes/西方经济学导论-笔记), [finance notes](/reference/notes/金融学-笔记) and [microeconomics notes](/reference/微观经济学-笔记).
