
# Introduction — Definition, Framework and Entry Paths to Finance

## 1. Defining and tracing the word "finance"

### (1) The concept of finance and its development

The collective term for circulation of money, credit activity, and the economic activity connected with them.

### (2) Western accounts of "finance"

The operation of capital markets and the supply and pricing of capital assets.

### (3) The revolutionary teachers, and the modern Chinese account of "finance"

Marx and Engels: money is the first driving force and the ultimate driving force.
Deng Xiaoping's theory of finance:
1. The central role of finance in allocating resources.
2. The important role of finance in regulating the economy.
3. Finance as the core of national economic security.

### (4) Grasping the real meaning of "finance"

1. One must not overlook that finance is principally financing activity, centered on serving the real economy.
2. Against the background of financial innovation, as financing activity develops and changes, the institutions, the scale, the settlement methods and the instruments of financing all keep advancing from the singular to the plural, from the simple to the complex.
3. Different angles tend to give different definitions.

Finance:
1. Narrow sense: the pooling of monetary funds.
2. Broad sense: finance involves not only the set of all monetary and credit transactions, but also the set of all transactions — in money supply, banking and non-banking credit, securities-traded investment, commercial insurance, and the like.

# Chapter 1 Money — the foundational element of finance

## Section 1 The "quality" of money: what money is

### Marx solves the "puzzle" of the essence of money

#### (1) The "puzzle" of money

Representative views:
1. The appearance of money is tied to exchange.
2. Money was established by rulers with power, or by the wise.

#### (2) Marx's scientific analysis of the essence of money

##### 1. The abstract-logical method

Only after commodity exchange appears, and as commodity exchange develops, does one commodity gradually separate itself from the world of commodities and serve as a fixed medium of exchange — that is money.

Two principles of commodity exchange:
1. The two commodities being exchanged must have different use-values.
2. The two commodities being exchanged must have equal values.

Four stages of the value form in the development of commodity exchange:
1. The simple or accidental form of value.
2. The total or expanded form of value.
3. The general form of value.
4. The monetary form of value.

##### 2. The historical method

All commodities share one common point: each is the product of expended general human labor, or what is called abstract labor.
This general, abstract labor congealed in a commodity is value. Because the values of different commodities are identical in kind, they can be compared in quantity.
Exchanging commodities of equal quantity of value is exchange at equivalent value. Value itself is an abstract existence and only finds expression in the relation of commodity exchange.
The process of expressing value is also the process by which private labor is converted into social labor.

1. Money arose spontaneously, to meet the needs of economic development.
2. A commodity's becoming money is a process of development from the lower to the higher.

## 2. Money as the universal equivalent, reflecting the production relations of a given social formation

### (1) The two basic features of money as the universal equivalent

1. Money is the instrument by which the value of every commodity is expressed.
2. Money has the ability to be exchanged directly with every commodity.

### (2) Money reflects the production relations of a given social formation

As countries accelerate the marketization and internationalization of their economic development, the currencies of different countries all come to express the relation of unity among commodity producers and operators — that is, the relations of production, exchange and distribution of commodities.

The social relations of production that money reflects should be closely tied to the particular social formation of a given age.

### 3. The functions of money are the concrete embodiment of its essence

The functions of money are the concrete embodiment of the essence of money.

In the course of the development of money's exchange with commodities, five functions gradually formed:
1. Measure of value (a basic function).
2. Means of circulation (a basic function).
3. Means of payment.
4. Store of value.
5. World money.

#### (1) Measure of value: expressing and measuring commodity value in price form

When money expresses the value of commodities and measures the magnitude of their quantity of value, it performs the function of measure of value.

Features of money performing the function of measure of value:
1. It is the external expression of the commodity's inner measure of value, namely labor time.
2. It may be an imaginary idea of money, but it must have full value.
3. It is wholly exclusive.
4. The function of measure of value is completed through the intermediate link of the standard of price.

As commodity economy develops, the monetary unit name separates from the monetary unit of weight.
1. The inflow of foreign money.
2. As wealth grows, base-metal coinage is replaced by precious metal.
3. The state mints money of insufficient value.

Measure of value and standard of price are two concepts strictly distinguished yet closely connected.

Differences:
1. The measure of value forms spontaneously in commodity exchange; the standard of price is prescribed by state law.
2. Gold as measure of value measures the value of commodities; fixing a certain quantity of gold as the standard of price is for comparing the different gold contents of commodities, and thereby measuring their different quantities of value.
3. As measure of value, the quantity of value of the monetary commodity changes with labor productivity; as standard of price it is the weight of the money itself, unrelated to productivity.

Connection:
The standard of price is a technical provision made so that money can perform its function of measure of value.

#### (2) Means of circulation: serving as the medium of commodity transactions

When money serves as the medium of commodity circulation, it performs the function of means of circulation.

Features of money performing the function of means of circulation:
1. It may be real money, but it need not be full-value money itself — it can be replaced by a token of money.
2. It contains the possibility of crisis.

#### (3) Means of payment: money transferred one-sidedly as an independent form of value

When money is transferred one-sidedly as an independent form of value, it performs the function of means of payment.

Features of money performing the function of means of payment, compared with means of circulation:
1. Money as means of circulation is the medium of commodity exchange; money as means of payment is the link that completes the exchange.
2. The means of circulation serves commodity circulation chiefly, whereas the means of payment, besides serving commodity circulation, serves a great deal of other economic behavior.
3. Insofar as both mediate commodity circulation, they are both general means of purchase, but the means of circulation is purchase for immediate settlement, while the means of payment is purchase across periods.
4. The means of circulation acts where no debt relation exists, while the means of payment acts where a debt relation exists.
5. The development of credit sales creates a long chain of payments among commodity producers; if one producer fails to pay on time, a chain reaction follows, and in severe cases large numbers of enterprises go bankrupt, causing a monetary crisis. So the appearance and spread of the means of payment created the objective conditions for economic crisis to turn from possibility into reality.

#### (4) Store of value: a "pause" in the purchasing power of money

When money, for various reasons, withdraws from circulation and is held as an independent form of value and the absolute embodiment of social wealth, money stops circulating and performs the function of store of value.

Features:
1. It must be both real money and full-value money. Money as store of value must be money in the solid sense.
2. Money as store of value must withdraw from the sphere of circulation and stand still.

Beyond serving as the absolute embodiment of social wealth, the function of hoarding money is strengthened in three ways:
1. Hoarding as a reserve for the means of circulation.
2. Hoarding as a reserve for the means of payment.
3. Hoarding as a reserve for world money.

Hoarded money has the special function of spontaneously regulating the quantity of money. The store of value is both the drain and the aqueduct of monetary circulation.

Whether paper money can be hoarded depends on whether it can stably represent a given quantity of value.

#### (5) World money: money acting as universal equivalent across national frontiers

With the development of international trade, when money crosses national frontiers and acts as the universal equivalent on the world market, it performs the function of world money.

Marx's scientific account of world money under the classic gold standard: money as world money returns to its original form as gold and silver.

Money as the universal equivalent — the two basic features distinguishing the universal equivalent from an ordinary commodity:
1. Money can express the value of every commodity.
2. It has the ability to be exchanged directly with every commodity.

### 4. The forms of money and the evolution of their functions

Course of development:
1. Name and substance identical.
2. Name and substance no longer identical.
3. Name and substance separated.

Forms of money:

#### (1) Commodity money

Features:
1. High and stable value.
2. Easily divisible in substance.
3. Easy to carry and keep.

#### (2) Token money

Advantages over commodity money:
1. Low minting cost.
2. Avoids wear and deliberate clipping.
3. Lower transport cost and risk.

#### (3) Credit money

Credit money is a medium of circulation backed by the credit guarantee of the state and the banks.

For credit money to serve as a general medium of exchange, two interrelated conditions are needed:
1. Legislative guarantee for the issue of money.
2. Confidence in that money among people.

Main forms of credit money:
1. Subsidiary coin.
2. Cash notes.
3. Bank deposits. A deposit is the depositor's claim on the bank; for the bank, this money is at the same time a debt money.
4. Electronic money.

## Section 2 The "quantity" of money: what counts as money

### 1. The view of western scholars

Money comprises two components:
1. Currency $C$ — cash outside the banking system, that is, the notes and change that governments and central banks issue and that the public and social bodies accept.
2. Demand deposits $D_d$ — the balances in current accounts at deposit-money banks.

$M_1$ denotes the money stock:

$$
  M_1 = C + D_d
$$

### 2. Marx on the scope of the money stock

#### (1) Marx distinguishes a "narrow" and a "broad" scope of the money stock

- 1. Narrow: gold --> metallic money --> coinage + bank notes
- 2. Broad: the forms of money can be many and varied.

#### (2) Why Marx drew the scope of the money stock narrow and broad

Marx took gold coinage — unrestricted at any time, able stably to serve as means of circulation and means of payment — as the foundation of circulation; but when studying real economic problems he could not but move from the "narrow" scope of money to the "broad" one. From a scientific analytic standpoint, this was a perfectly correct view grounded in the historical conditions of the time.

### 3. The partiality of the traditional Chinese scope of money, and its correction

#### (1) The partiality of the view, and its reach

The scope of money as people conceive it means cash.

Partial knowledge leads to partial practice, showing up as:
1. Refusing to count deposits in the sphere of transfer settlement as money, which in substance denies the existence of commodity production and commodity circulation in that sphere — at odds with the reality of the socialist market economy at the present stage of China.
2. Judging whether monetary circulation is normal by a single indicator: the ratio between the volume of cash circulation and the retail turnover of consumer goods.
3. Explaining price rises by a single cause, namely too much cash being issued, and therefore holding that restraining cash issue and organizing the collection of cash are the only way to stabilize prices.
4. Overlooking the expansion of deposit money.

#### (2) Correcting the partial view

A unified monetary circulation must not consist only of cash, or the "small monetary circulation", but at minimum include the "large monetary circulation" of both cash and deposits.

Reasons:
1. It helps observe the objective requirements of the laws governing monetary circulation.
2. It helps measure the state of money supply correctly and completely.
3. It helps truly solve the problems that exist throughout monetary circulation.

### 4. Tiers of the money stock

#### (1) What tiering of the money stock means

Tiering the money stock is a method of ordering the money in circulation chiefly according to the size of its liquidity, dividing it into several tiers and denoting each by a symbol.

#### (2) Liquidity — the basic basis of the tiering

Liquidity is the property of an asset being able to be turned into real purchasing power in good time.

Basis of China's money-aggregate series:
1. Tiering by the turnover speed of money.
2. Tiering by the height of the conversion rate of money.
3. Tiering by the liquidity of money.
	- It grasps precisely the differences among the various concrete forms of money in circulation, in the character of their movement or their degree of activity, and gives the central bank a clear structural map of monetary circulation for setting macro-financial policy.
	- Liquidity is relative to the swap of money for commodities, which helps grasp the correspondence between different monetary compositions and the broadly corresponding commodity compositions.
	- The liquidity of money also carries the central bank's capacity, on the basis of analyzing economic change, to control a given tier of money.

Leaning on liquidity as the main basis requires holding to two principles:
1. Reality.
2. Measurability.

$$
M_0 = Currency in circulation
$$

$$
M_1 = M_0 + Corporate settlement deposits + Demand deposits of institutions + Military demand deposits
$$

$$
M_2 = M_1 + Urban and rural savings deposits + Corporate time deposits
$$

$$
M_3 = M_0 + All bank deposits
$$

$$
M_1(Narrow money) = M_0(Currency in circulation) + Demand deposits
$$

$$
M_2(Broad money) = M_1 + Time deposits + Personal deposits + Other deposits
$$

#### (3) The focus of control across the tiers

What bears directly on the market is the money actually in circulation ($M_0$ and $M_1$).

Over the medium and long term, $M_2$ should be the focus.

1. $M_2$ is tied to banking activity far more closely than the other tiers and has an important effect on market supply and demand.
2. The central bank's degree of control over $M_2$ is much greater than its control over $M_0$ and $M_1$.
3. Relative to $M_0$ and $M_1$, the velocity of $M_2$ is comparatively stable, so its relation to total demand for money is the steadiest and easiest to handle in practice; this is of particular use in studying monetary circulation and in forecasting its future trend.
