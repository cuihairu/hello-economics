# The historical progression of money and banking

This is not a table of contents but the chain of advance in money and finance theory. The core is not memorising concepts but seeing clearly: people first tried to explain why money exists, then explained how banks create credit, then explained inflation and policy, and finally pushed the questions on to exchange rates, the international monetary system and internal–external equilibrium.

## The problem tree and the progress of finding answers

  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Money supply and demand theory](/monetary/货币供求理论)
- Sub-question 1.3: Why the price of funds takes the form of an interest rate.
  Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 2 · Interest rate theory](/monetary/利率理论)
- The progress of finding the answer:
  1. Classical monetary theory explained first: why money exists, and how the quantity of money affects prices.
     Short answer: money springs from the needs of exchange, and changes in the quantity of money affect the price level.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Money supply and demand theory](/monetary/货币供求理论)
  2. This was not enough to explain: why people hold money, and why there is an interest rate.
     Short answer: speaking only of the quantity of money does not account for money demand or for the mechanism by which interest rates form.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Money supply and demand theory](/monetary/货币供求理论) · [Chapter 2 · Interest rate theory](/monetary/利率理论)
  3. Keynes pushed the question forward to: money demand, liquidity preference and interest rates.
     Short answer: people hold money for transactions, precautionary and speculative motives, and the interest rate is shaped by liquidity preference.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Money supply and demand theory](/monetary/货币供求理论) · [Chapter 2 · Interest rate theory](/monetary/利率理论)
  4. Interest rate theory advanced further to: the price of funds and the choice among assets.
     Short answer: the interest rate is essentially the price of funds, and it also reflects the choice between different assets.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 2 · Interest rate theory](/monetary/利率理论)
  5. The conclusion of this round: money is not merely a medium of exchange but the core variable of the whole financial system.
     Short answer: money, money demand and interest rates together form the foundation of modern financial operations.
     Corresponding chapter: [Chapter 1 · Money supply and demand theory](/monetary/货币供求理论) · [Chapter 2 · Interest rate theory](/monetary/利率理论)

## 1. Question 1: Why money exists, and how money supply and demand form

### From classical monetary thought to metallic money theory

- Transitional figures:
  From Aristotle to the classical economists, the discussion of the functions of money forms the early source of this line.
- Influenced by:
  The starting point comes from exchange economies and the experience of commodity money.
- What was proposed:
  An explanation of how money differentiated itself out of commodity exchange.
- What it explains:
  Why society needs a universally accepted medium of exchange.
- Core ideas:
  Money springs from the widening of exchange, and unites the measures of value, the means of circulation and the means of storage.
- Advance over predecessors:
  It moved money from a mere symbol of political authority to an endogenous outcome of market exchange.
- What it left unsolved:
  It says too little about how money is created in the age of credit money.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Money supply and demand theory](/monetary/货币供求理论)

### Fisher's equation of exchange and the quantity theory of money

- Representative work:
  *The Purchasing Power of Money*.
- Influenced by:
  Built on the foundation of classical monetary thought.
- What was proposed:
  The modern form of the quantity theory of money.
- What it explains:
  The relation between changes in the quantity of money and the price level.
- Core ideas:
  Other things being equal, changes in the quantity of money correspond to changes in the price level.
- Advance over earlier monetary thought:
  It formalised and quantified the question of how money affects prices.
- What it left unsolved:
  It explains too little about why money demand changes, or the role of interest rates and asset choice.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Money supply and demand theory](/monetary/货币供求理论)

### Keynes and the theory of liquidity preference

- Representative work:
  *The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money*.
- Influenced by:
  Shaped jointly by classical monetary theory and the experience of the Great Depression, it is also a correction of the traditional quantity theory.
- What was proposed:
  The theory of liquidity preference.
- What it explains:
  Why people hold money, and why the interest rate matters.
- Core ideas:
  Money demand arises not only from transaction needs but also from precautionary and speculative motives.
- Advance over the quantity theory:
  It moved from "the quantity of money determines prices" to "money demand also changes with interest rates and expectations".
- Whom it directly corrected:
  It directly corrected the traditional quantity theory's overly stable, overly mechanical understanding of money demand.
- What it left unsolved:
  How the banking system creates money, and the mechanism of credit expansion in modern finance, still need finer explanation.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Money supply and demand theory](/monetary/货币供求理论) · [Interest rate theory](/monetary/利率理论)

## 2. Question 2: Why the banking system became the core of modern finance

### Commercial banking theory since the 20th century

- Transitional figures:
  Bagehot's writings on the lender of last resort and banking stability are the important intermediary from analysing banking business to analysing the banking system.
- Influenced by:
  Built on the widening division of labour between money and credit.
- What was proposed:
  A systematic analysis of banks as credit intermediaries.
- What it explains:
  Why saving can be converted into investment through banks.
- Core ideas:
  By taking deposits, granting loans, transforming maturities and diversifying risk, banks become the core of modern finance.
- Advance over pure monetary theory:
  It moved the question of money on to credit creation and the structure of financial intermediation.
- What it left unsolved:
  The expansion of the banking system also brings liquidity risk, moral hazard and systemic risk.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [The financial intermediary system](/monetary/金融中介体系)

### Financial deepening and financial market theory since the 20th century

- Influenced by:
  Driven by the development of modern capital markets and financial innovation.
- What was proposed:
  Analyses of the division of labour among financial markets, securitisation, off-balance-sheet business and more.
- What it explains:
  Why modern financing is done not only through banks but also through multi-tiered markets.
- Core ideas:
  The financial system is jointly constituted by banks and markets, with different instruments bearing different functions of risk and maturity allocation.
- Advance over traditional banking theory:
  It moved from a "bank-centred view" to a "whole-financial-system view".
- What it left unsolved:
  While financial innovation raises efficiency, it can also amplify systemic risk.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Financial markets](/monetary/金融市场)

### The development of financial regulation theory

- Whom it directly criticised:
  It targets the optimistic assumption that "the financial system can stabilise itself entirely by market discipline".
- Influenced by:
  Built on the experience of bank runs, financial crises and market failure.
- What was proposed:
  Frameworks such as deposit insurance, the lender of last resort and prudential regulation.
- What it explains:
  Why the financial system cannot rely on market self-discipline alone.
- Core ideas:
  The financial system carries externalities and systemic risk, and regulation is part of the conditions for stability.
- Advance over pure financial market theory:
  It moved the dimension from "financial efficiency" to "financial stability".
- What it left unsolved:
  Regulation that is too strict may suppress innovation, while regulation that is too loose may accumulate crises.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [The financial regulatory system](/monetary/金融监管体系)

## 3. Question 3: Why money affects inflation, output and macro stability

### The development of inflation theory

- Influenced by:
  Shaped at once by the quantity theory, Keynesianism and postwar empirical research.
- What was proposed:
  Explanations such as demand-pull inflation, cost-push inflation and structural inflation.
- What it explains:
  Why prices keep rising, and why deflation also appears.
- Core ideas:
  Price fluctuations are not caused by a single factor but by the joint action of money, demand, costs and expectations.
- Advance over the traditional quantity theory:
  It moved from a single monetary explanation to multi-factor analysis of inflation.
- What it left unsolved:
  Different inflation mechanisms are often interwoven in reality, and identifying them for policy is not easy.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Inflation and deflation](/monetary/通货膨胀与通货紧缩)

### Modern monetary policy theory after the mid-20th century

- Influenced by:
  Shaped jointly by Keynesianism, monetarism and later rules-based theory.
- What was proposed:
  Analysis of central bank objectives, instruments, intermediate targets and policy rules.
- What it explains:
  How the central bank stabilises the economy through money supply, interest rates and expectations management.
- Core ideas:
  Monetary policy affects demand, prices and financial conditions through multiple transmission channels, but with lags and uncertainty.
- Advance over early monetary theory:
  It moved from "money affects the economy" to "how the central bank systematically employs monetary instruments".
- What it left unsolved:
  How to balance rules against discretion has always been debated.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Monetary policy](/monetary/货币政策)

## 4. Question 4: In an open economy, how to understand exchange rates, the international monetary system and internal–external equilibrium

### Purchasing power parity and exchange rate theory

- Representative work:
  Cassel's writings on purchasing power parity.
- Influenced by:
  Built on the quantity theory of money and international price comparison.
- What was proposed:
  Exchange rate explanations such as purchasing power parity.
- What it explains:
  Why exchange rates change.
- Core ideas:
  In the long run the exchange rate is closely tied to each country's price level and monetary conditions.
- Advance over closed-economy monetary theory:
  It moved monetary theory on to cross-country price and monetary relations.
- What it left unsolved:
  It explains short-run exchange rate swings and capital-flow shocks too little.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Exchange rate theory](/monetary/汇率理论)

### The evolution of the international monetary system

- Whom it directly corrected:
  Each rebuilding of the international monetary system in effect corrected the failures of the previous regime in stability, liquidity or adjustment mechanisms.
- Influenced by:
  Built on the expansion of international trade and the changing arrangements for international payment.
- What was proposed:
  Institutional analyses of the gold standard, Bretton Woods, the Jamaica system and others.
- What it explains:
  How money is settled, reserved and stabilised at the global level.
- Core ideas:
  The international monetary system is not a natural occurrence but an institutional arrangement; every system trades off stability, liquidity and policy autonomy.
- Advance over pure exchange rate theory:
  It moved from "how the exchange rate forms" to "how the global monetary order is organised".
- What it left unsolved:
  The Triffin dilemma, reserve-currency dependence and global imbalances persist.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [The international monetary system](/monetary/国际货币体系)

### Internal–external equilibrium theory

- Influenced by:
  Directly shaped by open-economy macroeconomics, exchange rate theory and balance of payments theory.
- What was proposed:
  Policy-mix analysis of internal and external equilibrium.
- What it explains:
  How a country can at once maintain stable domestic employment and balance in its international payments.
- Core ideas:
  Fiscal policy, monetary policy and the exchange rate regime must be coordinated; a single instrument cannot solve all goals at once.
- Advance over closed-economy policy theory:
  It moved macroeconomic management from a domestic question to a multi-objective constrained problem in an open economy.
- What it left unsolved:
  Under high capital mobility, policy autonomy remains limited.
- Corresponding back to the textbook:
  [Internal–external equilibrium theory](/monetary/内外均衡理论)

## 5. Finally, compressing the whole book into one sentence

- The order of theoretical advance in money and banking is:
  first explain what money is, then how credit is created, then how money affects inflation and macro stability, and lastly the open economy and the international monetary order.
