# Chapter 1 · The essential features of the socialist economic system

> **The central question of this chapter**: where lies the essential difference between the socialist economic system and the capitalist system? What basic features did Marx envisage for the future society? Why should a China that never went through a full development of capitalism take the socialist road — and how?

## The arc of this chapter

The theoretical foundation of the socialist economic system is the scientific forecast Marx and Engels made of the future society. That forecast grew out of critique: utopian socialism judged capitalism from rational principles such as human fairness and justice, and on that basis conceived an ideal kingdom — Saint-Simon, Fourier and Owen are deserving of respect for their critique of private ownership and poverty and for their experiments with cooperative communities, but their fatal weakness was the lack of two things: a historical mechanism (why capitalism must necessarily be transcended) and a realistic path (by whom, and through what means, the new society is to be built). The contribution of Marx and Engels lay precisely in supplying both: through a deep analysis of the internal contradictions and motive laws of the capitalist mode of production they discovered, from within the disintegrating forms of economic motion, the elements of a new organization of production and exchange capable of removing those ills; and instead of drawing a complete blueprint of the future society in every detail, they understood it from the trend of human social development. The materialist conception of history plus the theory of surplus value turned socialism from a moral wish into a historical science.

The forecast became a system under entirely different historical conditions. Marx and Engels repeatedly stressed that their theory was not dogma but the point of departure and the method for further study; yet later followers turned the theory into doctrine, congealed it in the Soviet Union into a highly centralized planned-economy model, and spread it to the socialist countries as the 'standard answer'. China embarked on the socialist road on a foundation where capitalism had never fully developed — this specific social-historical condition inevitably shaped the choice of a socialist model: copying the highly centralized system was long taken as self-evident, and its friction with a backward, extremely unevenly developing productive base later became the deep motive of reform. The economic theory of socialism with Chinese characteristics took shape step by step precisely in the situation of 'the system already exists, but the model must be rewritten'. Its main points include: the theory of the primary stage of socialism — a scientific judgment of the historical position China occupies; the theory of the essence of socialism — in his 1992 southern tour talks Deng Xiaoping summed up the essence of socialism as liberating the productive forces, developing the productive forces, eliminating exploitation, removing polarization, and ultimately achieving common prosperity, pulling 'what is socialism' back from the level of institutional forms to the level of productive forces and the people's interests; the theory of economic reform — reform is the self-improvement of the socialist system, with the 'three favorables' as the criterion of judgment; the theory of the socialist market economy — plan and market are both economic instruments, not the mark distinguishing social systems; the theory of income distribution — income distribution depends on the distribution of the conditions of production, with the labor standard prevailing under public ownership and the ownership standard prevailing under non-public ownership; the theory of opening to the outside world — opening up is fixed as a basic national policy; and the scientific outlook on development that is people-oriented, comprehensive, coordinated and sustainable. The concepts section of this chapter gives two props: the primary stage and the basic economic system.

## Core concepts

### The primary stage of socialism

The primary stage of socialism refers to the specific historical stage that China must necessarily pass through in building socialism under conditions of backward productive forces and an underdeveloped commodity economy — that is, the entire historical stage from China's entry into socialism to the basic realization of socialist modernization. It contains two layers of meaning: first, China has already entered socialist society and must adhere to socialism, not depart from it; second, China's socialism is still at an underdeveloped stage, and this stage must be faced squarely, not skipped over.

Why is this concept needed? Because it defends against two errors at once: the essence of the 'Left' error is skipping over stages — imagining that the policies of the advanced stage can be imposed directly, bypassing the necessary process of developing the productive forces; the essence of the Right error is denying the social nature of China — imagining that, being underdeveloped, one might as well abandon the socialist direction. Taken together, the two layers of meaning fix a double coordinate of 'what it is' and 'what must not be done' for all current policies. It is also the key to understanding the later chapters: precisely because China is in the primary stage, multiple forms of ownership must coexist (Chapter 1), the market mechanism must be used (Chapter 2), and reform must be gradual (Chapter 3).

### The 'three favorables' criterion

The 'three favorables' criterion takes as the standard for judging right and wrong, gain and loss, in reform and in all other work whether it favors developing the productive forces of socialist society, whether it favors strengthening the comprehensive national power of the socialist state, and whether it favors raising the people's living standards. Deng Xiaoping advanced it in early 1992, in his southern tour talks, against the controversy over 'capitalism or socialism'.

Why is this criterion needed? In the course of reform, almost every new measure (the household contract responsibility system, wage labor, the stock market, the special economic zones) met the challenge 'is this capitalism or socialism?'; without an operational standard of judgment, reform would have run aground in endless ideological debate. The 'three favorables' move the anchor of judgment from 'what it looks like in form' to 'what it delivers in result' — productive forces, comprehensive national power and living standards are all observable, comparable outcome indicators. The three criteria form a whole: the first argues from the height of the productive forces, the second from the height of state capacity, the third from the height of the people's interest; together they cover the three dimensions of 'efficiency — state — people' and prevent a single indicator (say, GDP alone) from substituting for a comprehensive judgment.

### The collective economy

The collective economy is a form of public ownership in which part of the working masses jointly own the means of production. In a collective-ownership economy the workers jointly own the means of production within one collective, achieving equality in the ownership relation with respect to that collective's means of production; but among the various collective economic organizations de facto inequality in the ownership of the means of production remains, and from this arise marked differences in the appropriation of the fruits of labor and in the distribution of personal income — this is the important feature distinguishing collective ownership from ownership by the whole people.

Why is this structure of 'equality within the collective, inequality among collectives' worth pointing out specifically? Because it depicts exactly the layered character of public ownership: the wider the scope of publicization, the broader the coverage of equality, but the harder the problems of incentive and information. The boundary of collective ownership is drawn at 'the real unit of common labor', tying the ownership relation directly to the perceivable interests of the members — the rise of township-and-village enterprises after the rural household contract system, and the practice of community collective economies, both rest on this property-rights layer.

### The mixed-ownership economy

The mixed-ownership economy is an economic form composed of ownership economies of different natures. With the formation and development of the ownership structure in which public ownership is the mainstay and ownership economies of many kinds develop together, the various ownership economies blend, cross-hold shares and hold equity in one another, giving rise to this new form of ownership.

The significance of developing mixed ownership: it helps enterprises transform their operating mechanisms and form, inside the enterprise, a management mechanism in which powers, responsibilities and interests are unified and mutually restraining; it helps diversify investors and strengthens the enterprise's financial capacity and scale; it helps further expand opening to the outside world, absorbing foreign capital and advanced technology and management experience; and it helps enterprises build up capital and technological strength and enhance market competitiveness. Why is mixed ownership regarded as 'an important realization form of the basic economic system'? Because it pushes 'the coexistence of different forms of ownership' from market-based division of labor outside the enterprise to the property-rights structure inside it — public and non-public components coexist and develop together within the same enterprise, and this in turn pushes the public component to raise its quality and vitality in market competition.

## Questions and analysis

### What forecasts did Marx and Engels make about the basic features of the future society?

Marx's and Engels's analysis and summary of the basic economic features of the future society can be reduced to five points.

First, the association of free individuals and the free and all-round development of the individual. Only when a person becomes master of society, of nature and of themselves can they become a free person; only an association composed of free individuals is an association of free individuals. The free and all-round development of the human being is both the objective trend of human social development and an important goal of the future society. The real realization of individual freedom cannot be separated from the collective — only conscious social regulation of production, resting on social ownership of the means of production, allows human society to pass truly from the realm of necessity into the realm of freedom.

Second, replacing private ownership with public ownership of the means of production. Class antagonism and the anarchy of social development in capitalist society are caused by private ownership of the means of production; abolishing private ownership is the objective requirement of the development of the productive forces and the fundamental path to building the association of free individuals. Only social ownership of the means of production and planned production on that basis can resolve the basic contradiction of capitalism at its root.

Third, developing the productive forces as fast as possible and achieving common prosperity. To develop the productive forces as rapidly as possible so as to satisfy the ever-growing needs of the masses is the basic purpose of production in the future society, and reflects the fundamental nature of that society.

Fourth, abolishing commodity production and subjecting all production to planned regulation. In the envisaged future society, because public ownership of the means of production prevails, labor becomes directly social labor, and 'the socially planned distribution of labor time' becomes possible.

Fifth, distribution according to labor and distribution according to need in the two phases of communism respectively. On the basis of the different natures of the productive forces Marx divided communist society into a lower and a higher phase: the lower phase practices distribution according to labor, the higher phase distribution according to need. Socialism, as the lower phase of communism, carries the general features of communism while having its own specific determinations.

Reading the five points as one chain reveals a rigorous logic: the goal is the free and all-round development of the person, the means is social ownership of the means of production, the premise is the high development of the productive forces, the allocation method is planned regulation, and distribution proceeds in two steps according to maturity. Precisely for that reason, every later deviation in practice (the retention of commodity-money relations, the real form of distribution according to labor, the information and incentive problems of planning) became the starting point that socialist economic theory had to rethink — which is the theme of Chapters 2 and 3.

### What are the meaning and significance of the theory of the primary stage of socialism?

On the meaning: the primary stage of socialism refers to the specific stage China must pass through in building socialism under conditions of backward productive forces and an underdeveloped commodity economy — the entire historical stage from entering socialism to basically realizing socialist modernization. Its two layers of meaning — already in socialist society, still at an underdeveloped stage — must be grasped simultaneously: stressing only the first leads to rejecting all transitional retreats and to the 'Left' error of skipping stages; stressing only the second shakes the direction of the basic system and commits the Right error of denying the social nature.

On the significance: first, building socialism must proceed from national conditions, and the primary stage is the largest national condition, the most basic reality, the general basis for drawing up the line, principles and policies — why the economic construction center is adhered to, why multiple forms of ownership are allowed to develop, why reform proceeds gradually, all must be explained from the fact of the 'underdeveloped stage'. Second, confirming that China is and will long remain in the primary stage of socialism is the point of departure and the footing of the theory and practice of building socialism with Chinese characteristics — it moves the theory from normative argument about 'what ought to be' to positive analysis of 'what is possible under what conditions', and that is the institutional foundation of the methodological turn.

### How should the relationship between the public-ownership economy and the non-public-ownership economy be correctly understood?

First, the public-ownership economy occupies the mainstay position in the basic economic system, and that mainstay position has both quantitative and qualitative determinations. Quantitatively, public assets must hold the dominant share in total social assets; qualitatively, the mainstay position should be embodied in the control of the lifelines of the national economy and the guiding role in economic development by the public-ownership economy, state-owned above all. Upholding the mainstay position at the present stage requires not only maintaining the quantitative advantage but stressing qualitative improvement — raising control and influence and giving play to the guiding role. The qualitative determination is stressed because a purely quantitative advantage, if it exists in the form of low-efficiency assets, would instead drag down the economy as a whole; the formulation 'control and guidance' converts a 'proportion question' into a 'structure question'.

Second, upholding the mainstay position of public ownership requires giving play to the leading role of the state-owned economy. The key of the leading role does not lie in the absolute quantitative dominance and monopoly position of state-owned enterprises, but in the state-owned economy using its relative advantages and specific functions to raise its control, competitiveness and influence. To this end the layout and structure of the state-owned economy must be adjusted according to the requirements of the development of the productive forces; the general principle is: advance in some areas and retreat in others, do what should be done and refrain from what should not, concentrate forces, strengthen the key points, and raise the overall quality of the state-owned economy. This principle supplies the general outline for the classified reform strategy of Chapter 4 (differentiated treatment of public goods, natural monopoly and competitive sectors).

Third, develop not only public-ownership economies of many forms but also non-public-ownership economies of many forms. Individual, private and foreign-funded economies play an irreplaceable role in employment, in the diversification of products and services, and in competitive vitality, and are highly compatible with the market economy. Ownership economies of many kinds each have their features, advantages and limitations; under the present state of the productive forces each has fields in which its advantages are best exercised, and they should be rationally arranged according to their features so that their functions complement one another; competition among them favors efficiency, technical progress and the adjustment of the ownership structure.

## Synthetic essays

### The basic content of the economic theory of socialism with Chinese characteristics

**(1) The theory of the primary stage of socialism**. After the Third Plenary Session of the Eleventh Central Committee the Party summed up the experience and lessons of socialist construction, re-examined the historical stage China occupies, and advanced the judgment that China is still in the primary stage of socialism. This judgment shows, on the one hand, that China has already entered socialist society and, on the other, that China's socialism is still at an underdeveloped stage — it is the scientific judgment of contemporary China's basic national conditions and the general basis of all theory and policy.

**(2) The theory of the essence of socialism and of ownership relations**. In his 1992 southern tour talks Deng Xiaoping gave a new summary of the essence of socialism: liberating the productive forces, developing the productive forces, eliminating exploitation, removing polarization, and ultimately achieving common prosperity. The first three clauses concern the productive forces and the means of development, the last two concern production relations and the goal of distribution — the essence is defined in function and result, not in some fixed institutional form. This opened space for institutional exploration (the market, the share system, multiple forms of ownership) and set the value standard for adjusting ownership relations.

**(3) The theory of economic reform**. The nature of reform is not a negation of the socialist system but the self-improvement and self-development of the socialist system; reform is also a new revolution — not patching up minor details of the original system but changing, at the root, the traditional economic system that fettered the development of the productive forces. The goal of reform is to establish a socialist market economy system; in content, institutional reform is combined with institutional innovation to achieve the combination of socialism and the market economy; in method, reform proceeds from reality, advances as a whole, breaks through at key points and moves step by step; strategically it balances reform, development and stability; and the criterion of its success is the 'three favorables'.

**(4) The theory of the socialist market economy**. The socialist market economy is a new type of system combining the market economy with socialism, in which the market mechanism plays the basic role in resource allocation under state macro-control. The theory and policy formed around this goal include: public ownership as the mainstay with diverse forms of ownership developing together; distribution according to labor as the mainstay with diverse modes of distribution coexisting; the realization forms of public ownership can and should be diversified; establishing the modern enterprise system; correctly handling the relations between central and local and bringing the two initiatives into play; and combining the market mechanism with macro-control.

**(5) The theory of socialist income distribution**. Income distribution depends on the distribution of the conditions of production. Under the socialist market economy the distribution of the conditions of production has changed greatly, and the modes and structure of income distribution have changed with it: the labor standard prevails mainly in the public-ownership economy, the ownership standard mainly in the non-public-ownership economy; combining the labor standard with the ownership standard, and distribution according to labor with distribution according to factors of production, the diverse modes of distribution interpenetrate, influence and complement one another, forming an income distribution structure suited to China's socialist market economy. This theory is developed in Chapter 6.

**(6) The theory of opening to the outside world**. Under conditions of rapidly developing economic globalization, whether to join the globalization process, how to open up, and how to use the two markets and two kinds of resources effectively under openness to accelerate development are both major theoretical and major practical questions of socialism. China has fixed opening to the outside world as a basic national policy of building socialism with Chinese characteristics — the basis of opening is not expediency but a theoretical understanding of the gains from division of labor and the constraint of international resources (developed in Chapter 9).

**(7) The theory of people-oriented scientific development**. Guided by Deng Xiaoping Theory and the important thought of the 'Three Represents', the Party summed up domestic and foreign experience and lessons on development and advanced the scientific outlook on development, which puts people first and is comprehensive, coordinated and sustainable. The scientific outlook on development further clarified the essential goal, basic content, general approach, mode choice and fundamental driving force of China's economic and social development; it is a scientific understanding of the laws of economic and social development under the socialist market economy and a major innovation of Marxist development theory (developed in Chapter 7).

The economic theory of socialism with Chinese characteristics is summarized from practice and reflects the basic experience of socialist construction since reform and opening up, and the understanding of the laws of economic construction under new historical conditions; it has the theoretical quality of advancing with the times and will keep enriching itself as practice develops and the understanding of the laws of the socialist economy deepens.

### The basic economic system of the primary stage of socialism

**(1) The content and basis of the system**. The ownership of the means of production is the foundation of the social relations of production, and the rationalization of the ownership structure has fundamental significance for developing the productive forces and perfecting the socialist system. Public ownership as the mainstay with diverse forms of ownership developing together is a basic economic system suited to China's national conditions. Its basis is precisely the state of the productive forces in the primary stage: productive forces that are underdeveloped and extremely uneven objectively require ownership forms of different levels to match them — the full coverage of a single public ownership is both inefficient and beyond the stage.

**(2) The mainstay position of public ownership**. The mainstay position carries a double determination of quantity and quality: quantitatively, public assets hold the dominant share in total social assets; qualitatively, the state-owned economy controls the lifelines of the national economy and plays a guiding role in economic development. The end-point of upholding the mainstay position is giving play to the leading role of the state-owned economy; layout adjustment follows the principle of 'advancing in some areas and retreating in others, doing what should be done and refraining from what should not', concentrating state capital in the important industries and key fields that concern the lifelines of the national economy and national security.

**(3) The joint development of diverse ownership economies**. Non-public economies — individual, private, foreign-funded — play an irreplaceable role in expanding employment, meeting diverse demands and strengthening competitive vitality. The relationship between the public-ownership economy and the non-public-ownership economy is not one of mutual separation and mutual exclusion but of each giving play to its own advantages in market competition, mutual promotion and common development; the mixed-ownership economy is the embodiment of this fusion in the internal form of the enterprise.

**(4) The diversification of the realization forms of public ownership**. In the course of reform and opening up, many realization forms of public ownership have appeared in China: the cooperative economy, the share economy, the share-cooperative system, and the mixed-ownership economy. Realization form and nature of ownership are two different levels of question — the same public ownership can have many realization forms, and the same realization form (such as the share system) can also carry different contents of ownership. This distinction is the premise, in ownership theory, of the share-system transformation of state-owned enterprises (Chapter 4).

## Limits and what follows

The theory of this chapter has seen four important lines of development in later practice and literature; read the textbook with these coordinates in mind. First, **the dynamization of the primary-stage thesis**: the primary stage of socialism is a long historical process (when the Thirteenth Congress expounded it systematically in 1987 it was estimated to last a hundred years and more), but within it there are phase changes — in 2017 the Nineteenth Congress announced that socialism with Chinese characteristics had entered a new era and that the principal social contradiction had transformed into the contradiction between the people's ever-growing needs for a better life and unbalanced and inadequate development; this is a re-description of the features of the stage within the primary-stage framework, not a replacement of it. Second, **the promotion and extension of the basic economic system**: in 2019 the Fourth Plenary Session of the Nineteenth Central Committee set side by side 'public ownership as the mainstay with diverse forms of ownership developing together, distribution according to labor as the mainstay with diverse modes of distribution coexisting, and the socialist market economy system', calling all three together the basic socialist economic system — the extension of the institution expanded from ownership to distribution and the mode of resource allocation, and a textbook read only through the old tripartite scheme will underestimate its scope. Third, **the continued reiteration of the 'two unwaverings' and the tension in practice**: the '56789' summary of the private economy's contribution (over 50% of tax revenue, over 60% of GDP, over 70% of technological innovation, over 80% of urban employment, over 90% of the number of enterprises — a common summary in policy documents; specific figures are subject to the latest official statistics) coexists with real differences in market access and financing conditions, and the relation between the mainstay position and common development remains an object of repeated theoretical and policy adjustment. Fourth, **unresolved theoretical disputes**: on the quantitative standard of the 'mainstay position of public ownership' (the share of state capital, the share of employment, or control indicators) and on the trade-off between control and efficiency in mixed-ownership reform, academia and policy departments still differ — these open questions are precisely why the concepts of this chapter continue to be used and refined.
